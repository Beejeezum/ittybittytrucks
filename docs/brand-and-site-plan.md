# Itty Bitty Trucks: brand and site plan

## The idea

**You found an itty bitty truck.**

Build the experience around the moment someone spots a little Japanese truck, smiles, and wants to know what it is. Help that person meet the actual truck, understand ownership, and start looking for one of their own. Casual curiosity should feel welcome; serious purchase interest should have an obvious next step.

The canonical domain is **ittybittytrucks.com**. Redirect **ittybittytruck.com** to the plural domain, preserving meaningful paths. Keep the individual encounter URL **/hi/teal** stable so a printed QR code remains useful as the site grows.

## Voice and visual direction

The brand speaks as a friendly, knowledgeable human. Individual truck profiles get a little first-person personality. Specifications, pricing, availability, and buying information stay plain and precise.

Let the trucks supply the cuteness through their proportions, colors, and everyday usefulness. Use large real photographs, confident typography, generous space, warm white, near-black, and a strong accent color. Individual vehicle colors can become profile accents. Keep buttons and text comfortably readable on a phone beside a parked truck.

Prefer real owner-supplied photography. Carefully masking an actual truck photograph is appropriate when its proportions and appearance remain intact. Avoid generated photorealistic trucks, excessive stickers, random Japanese graphics, baby talk, stock dealership imagery, and ornamental animation. Any illustration should look intentionally illustrated and serve a clear purpose.

## Site structure

| Destination | Purpose |

| The trucks | Show individual profiles with explicit, confirmed status. |
| /hi/teal | Introduce Bruce's turquoise 2000 Subaru Sambar TT2 to QR visitors. |
| About kei trucks | Explain the vehicles and answer researched ownership questions. |
| Find me one | Collect a useful, nonbinding request. |
| About Bruce / contact | Establish who is behind the project and how to reach him. |

The first draft can combine most destinations into clear homepage sections. The truck encounter still needs its own direct entry. Keep navigation short: **The trucks · About kei trucks · Find me one**.

## Homepage copy

### Hero

**You found an itty bitty truck.**

Hello. These are kei trucks: compact Japanese pickups with an unreasonable amount of personality. Meet the trucks, get your questions answered, or tell us what you'd love to find.

Primary action: **Meet the trucks**. Secondary action: **I want one**.

### Truck introduction

**A few little introductions.**

Start with Bruce's actual Sambar. Use its confirmed year, model, color, owner, photographs, and a **Meet this truck** action. Add additional vehicles only when their details and public status are established.

### Learning section

**First question: why so small?**

Answer the immediate curiosity briefly, then offer practical questions: What can you use one for? What is it like to drive? What equipment is available? What does maintaining an older imported vehicle involve? How do you check whether one works for your location and intended use?

Research factual answers before publishing. Explain equipment as vehicle-specific where appropriate.

### Request invitation

**Thinking about your own?**

Maybe you know exactly what you want. Maybe you've only just learned these exist. Tell us a little about your plans, and we can start there.

Action: **Help me find a truck**.

### Founder introduction

**A little introduction from Bruce.**

I'm Bruce. I like little trucks, good design, and vehicles that make an ordinary errand more fun. Itty Bitty Trucks is a place to meet these things, learn what owning one involves, and start looking for your own.

## The Sambar encounter: /hi/teal

Identifier: **2000 Subaru Sambar TT2 · Bruce's truck**

Headline: **Oh, hey. You found me.**

Introduction: **I'm Bruce's turquoise Subaru Sambar. Currently making ordinary errands in South Florida a little more interesting.**

Actions: **Get to know me** and **I want one like this**. The second should prefill the inquiry with this truck as the visitor's reference.

Follow with verified specifications, a short owner-approved story, actual photographs, and a few relevant questions. A single playful line can sit beside the formal model name: **My name is long. The truck is not.** Keep personal-history claims subject to Bruce's review.

## Inventory and inquiry requirements

Public sale listings require explicit availability confirmation. Bruce's Sambar can appear as an owner profile while its sale status remains undecided. Keep the potential orange and cream Honda Acty unpublished until purchase and listing details are confirmed.

Before listing, collect actual photographs; year, make, model, and chassis details; odometer reading and units; transmission, drivetrain, and equipment; known condition and defects; asking price; location; title and import-document status; and the intended inquiry process. Identify estimates and pending work clearly. Avoid blanket registration, street-legality, highway, delivery, or road-readiness promises.

Keep the request form short: name, email, location, approximate total budget, intended use, timing, and must-haves or notes. Phone is optional. Use **Send my truck wish list** as the button. Publish a success message only after a real submission succeeds. A proposed public address is **hello@ittybittytrucks.com**; activate contact links after the mailbox exists.

## Future refinements

- Give each truck its own permanent QR introduction and small amount of personality.
- Add real scale photographs beside familiar vehicles or everyday cargo; verify dimensions and payload claims.
- Offer a quiet **Saw me somewhere? Say hi.** form for a general place and optional private note.
- Preserve profiles as their stories develop, updating ownership and availability accurately.

Prioritize an excellent introduction, useful information, and a functioning inquiry path. Add playful features when they strengthen those purposes.
