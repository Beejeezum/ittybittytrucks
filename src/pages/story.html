I bought a 2000 Subaru Sambar partly because I wanted to learn how to look after it. That turned into a much bigger interest in very small trucks.

01 / The truck that started it

Japan to Florida.
With a detour
through 1957.

I bought the Sambar in August 2025. It shipped from Japan that November and arrived through Jacksonville in December. Its next chapter was here in Boca Raton.

The look had already taken shape: Tropical Turquoise inspired by a 1957 Chevy, a black bumper, and white wheels. A Japanese work truck with a color scheme all its own.

The truck got its own corner of the internet as Tiny Teal Truck. Itty Bitty Trucks makes room for the idea to grow.

Meet the original

02 / Learning as an owner

Small truck.
Plenty to learn.

I had questions about bringing a truck over from Japan, the paperwork, parts, and how to take care of it once it was here. I’ve spent time tracking down a fuel hose and asking how to approach the work.

That experience shapes the way I want to introduce these trucks. The useful details deserve as much attention as the color: what you’re looking at, what it needs, and whether it fits the life you have in mind.

The introduction I would have wanted

03 / A few carefully chosen trucks

A little room
for personality.

I’m building Itty Bitty Trucks to import and sell a few carefully chosen kei trucks at a time. I’m drawn to beach-inspired colors, interesting combinations, and the details that make a truck feel like somebody’s truck.

Each one should get a proper introduction: its own photographs, known condition, equipment, location, price, and paperwork. That’s what you’ll find here when a truck is offered for sale.

For now, meet the Sambar, explore the guide, and start thinking about your own. Whether you’re ready to look or just stopped to say, “What is that thing?”, welcome.
