01 / What is it?

First question:
why so small?

These are kei trucks: compact Japanese utility pickups from Japan’s light-vehicle category. Their small proportions are the whole idea—an easy-to-maneuver little cab with a useful load bed behind it.

“Kei” describes a vehicle category, rather than one particular brand. Subaru Sambar, Honda Acty, Suzuki Carry, and Daihatsu Hijet are some of the names you’ll come across.

How tiny are we talking?

The kei standard introduced in October 1998 caps size at 3.4 meters long, 1.48 meters wide, and 2 meters tall, with an engine up to 660cc. That’s roughly 11 feet 2 inches long and 4 feet 10 inches wide. These are class limits; a particular truck’s actual measurements belong on its own listing.

Sources: Japan Light Motor Vehicle and Motorcycle Association: kei standards · Subaru’s Sambar introduction and equipment table.

02 / What’s it for?

Little errands.
Very good company.

Think garden-center runs, tools, a bicycle, or a project that could use an open bed. Your plans are a good place to start when you’re choosing a truck.

The important details are specific to the vehicle: bed dimensions, payload, drivetrain, condition, and where you plan to use it. We’ll put those details alongside each truck when it’s offered for sale.

03 / The little details

Same small idea.
Different little trucks.

Can I get air conditioning?

Make A/C a must-have on your wish list. Equipment varies by truck, and a listing should confirm both that it’s fitted and that it works.

What about an automatic or four-wheel drive?

Put your transmission and drivetrain preferences on the list too. Availability depends on the particular model, year, and vehicle. A model name alone isn’t a promise about its equipment.

What is driving on the right like?

Many Japanese-market trucks put the driver on the right. Ask about visibility, driving position, and a test drive when you assess a particular truck.

How fast does it go?

That’s a question for the specific truck and its condition. Tell us the roads and journeys you have in mind. A top-speed claim on its own doesn’t tell you whether a vehicle suits those trips.

04 / Before you buy

A little homework
goes a long way.

An older imported truck deserves the same close look as any older vehicle: its history, rust, mechanical condition, parts and service options, and the paperwork that comes with it.

Import eligibility and road use are separate questions.

A federal age-based import exemption does not, by itself, settle state registration, insurance, or the roads a particular truck may use. Confirm the exact vehicle’s documentation and your intended use before committing.

  • Confirm the manufacturing date and import documents.
  • Check the current title and registration status.
  • Ask your state motor-vehicle authority about your vehicle and intended use.
  • Confirm insurance and arrange a mechanical inspection.

Official starting points: NHTSA vehicle imports · EPA vehicle imports · Florida Highway Safety and Motor Vehicles.
